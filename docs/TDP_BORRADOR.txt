--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Abstract
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
We present Base@Home [POR CONFIRMAR: confirmar si el nombre oficial del robot
para 2027 es Base@Home u otro], the custom open-platform domestic service robot
developed by team UruBots (Uruguay) for the RoboCup@Home Open Platform League
(OPL). Built to maximize mechanical reach and reproducibility on a constrained
budget, the platform combines a differential-drive base powered by repurposed
6.5-inch brushless hub motors with custom Field-Oriented Control (FOC)
firmware, a 0.60 m working-stroke NEMA 23 prismatic torso elevator, a
side-mounted 6-DOF AgileX Piper manipulator with an eye-in-hand Orbbec DaBai
DC1 camera, and a 2-DOF Dynamixel pan-tilt head carrying an Intel RealSense
D435 RGB-D sensor and a directional shotgun microphone. Computation is
distributed across an Intel Core i3-1220P mini-PC (NUC) and an NVIDIA Jetson
Orin Nano 8 GB Super linked via point-to-point Gigabit Ethernet running ROS 2
Jazzy and unicast CycloneDDS. This paper details three core engineering
contributions: (1) a deterministic bilingual (English/Spanish) natural-language
compiler and supervised mission executor (`commander` and `mission_executor`)
paired with a streaming Silero VAD and Whisper ASR pipeline that eliminates
unconstrained Large Language Model (LLM) hallucinations from the actuator
control loop; (2) a coordinated floor-to-table manipulation architecture that
combines human pointing-gesture raycasting, open-vocabulary YOLO-World bag
detection, prismatic elevator positioning, and multi-orientation MoveIt 2
inverse kinematics; and (3) a hardware-safety-first actuator control layer and
lightweight multi-fidelity simulation stack (`base_athome_sim`) verified by
over 300 automated unit and integration tests. We report on-device perceptual
and acoustic benchmarks alongside simulation mission evaluations and explicitly
delineate the physical hardware validations currently in progress.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Introduction
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Domestic service robots operating in unstructured human environments must
integrate safe indoor navigation, spoken language interaction, 3D scene
understanding, and mobile manipulation across a wide vertical workspace ranging
from floor level to tabletops and shelves. Within the RoboCup@Home Open
Platform League (OPL) [1], teams face the dual challenge of achieving high
system reliability under strict time and safety constraints while keeping custom
hardware maintainable and reproducible.

Team UruBots [POR CONFIRMAR: breve reseña de 2–3 líneas sobre la historia del
equipo, institución y participaciones previas en competencias locales o
internacionales] developed Base@Home as a modular, open-source ROS 2 Jazzy
service robot designed around three guiding principles:
1. Measure first, correct second, and demonstrate with explicit evidence:
   Software modules enforce strict separation between measured sensor feedback
   and commanded states. Actuator controllers start disabled by default,
   reject motion commands when hardware feedback is stale or absent, and never
   publish synthetic zero joint states when sensors are disconnected.
2. Deterministic safety in the mission control loop: Rather than connecting
   generative Large Language Models (LLMs) directly to robot actuators—which
   risks unverified tool calls, unpredictable latency, or unsafe motion
   sequences—we compile spoken or typed requests through a deterministic
   grammar and semantic-map validator into structured execution plans with
   explicit preconditions, per-step timeouts, bounded retries, and spoken
   user confirmation before any physical motion occurs.
3. Peripheral-local distributed computing: Every sensor and actuator driver
   executes on the onboard computer physically attached to its hardware
   interface, connected over a wired Gigabit Ethernet link with unicast DDS
   discovery, ensuring compliance with RoboCup@Home wireless restrictions
   (RuleBook §3.2.8) and avoiding raw RGB-D video streaming across the network.

The main contributions described in this paper are:
- A complete, low-cost OPL hardware and distributed ROS 2 Jazzy architecture
  combining a hoverboard-based differential drive, a 0.60 m prismatic torso
  column, a side-mounted 6-DOF Piper arm, and a pan-tilt RGB-D head (Sections 2
  and 3).
- A deterministic voice-to-mission pipeline (`commander`, `mission_executor`,
  and request-indexed `whisper_node` with streaming Silero VAD) that translates
  natural-language commands into verified plans with spoken confirmation and
  cancellation (Section 4.1).
- A floor-to-table perception and manipulation pipeline integrating 3D human
  pointing-gesture raycasting, open-vocabulary YOLO-World bag detection,
  measured prismatic lift control (`torso_state`), and multi-orientation
  MoveIt 2 grasp planning with closed-loop `FollowJointTrajectory` execution
  (Section 4.2).
- A protected navigation stack with an offline-editable semantic object/zone
  layer and a zero-WiFi WebXR teleoperation pipeline for demonstration
  collection (Section 4.3).

2.1 Mechanical Structure, Mobile Base, and Prismatic Elevator
Base@Home is built on a square chassis measuring 0.48 m x 0.48 m in footprint
and 0.22 m in height (measured from the bottom face to the top mounting plate,
with 0.04 m ground clearance and `base_footprint` located 0.06 m below
`base_link`). Differential traction is provided by two 6.5-inch brushless DC
(BLDC) hub motors with integrated Hall-effect sensors (15 pole pairs x 6 states
= 90 ticks per wheel revolution), calibrated to an effective wheel radius of
0.0853 m under robot load and an effective kinematic track width of 0.5620 m
(5.95 mm per Hall tick). Rear support is provided by a caster wheel of 0.04 m
radius at x = -0.20 m. The motors are driven by an STM32F103 controller board
running custom Field-Oriented Control (FOC) firmware over a 115200 baud UART
link at 50 Hz.

To enable manipulation both on the floor (e.g., picking up pointed garbage
bags) and on standard domestic furniture without an excessively heavy arm, a
1.30 m vertical structural column (0.06 m profile width) is mounted at the
center of the base. A NEMA 23 stepper motor (200 steps/rev with 1/8
microstepping = 1600 steps/rev) controlled by an ESP32-S3 microcontroller drives
a vertical carriage (`torso_lift_link`) equipped with dual hardware limit
switches: a top endstop (GPIO Pin 7) and a bottom endstop (GPIO Pin 8) featuring
two-stage hardware debounce (500 us + 1 ms) and automatic safety cutoff. The
calibrated working stroke between limit switches is 0.60 m (positioning the
bottom of the torso prism between 1.00 m and 0.40 m above the floor, with a
maximum mechanical stroke of ~0.75 m). Mounted on the elevator carriage is a
0.25 m x 0.35 m x 0.35 m structural torso prism carrying a front-facing
0.26 m x 0.18 m interactive facial expression display and speaker.

2.2 Side-Mounted 6-DOF Manipulator and Eye-in-Hand Sensor
An AgileX Piper 6-DOF robotic arm (1.5 kg payload, ~626 mm reach, parallel
gripper) is mounted sideways on the left (+Y) vertical face of the moving torso
prism (`cube_to_arm_joint` offset xyz = (-0.15, 0.175, -0.11) m, rpy =
(-pi/2, 0, 0) rad), so that the base Z-axis of the arm points outward (+Y) and
its Y-axis points downward (-Z) [POR CONFIRMAR: verificación con cinta métrica
del offset físico exacto de la brida respecto a la columna]. With the elevator
at its top endstop (`torso_lift_joint` = 0.0 m), the shoulder origin
`arm_base_link` is located at (0.005, 0.175, 1.065) m in `base_link` (1.125 m
above the floor) and descends linearly down to z = 0.465 m at -0.60 m stroke.
This lateral mounting allows the arm to hang vertically alongside the torso in
its natural gravity rest configuration (`[86.97, 172.87, -162.63, -99.89,
-0.68, 44.98]` deg, measured via CAN bus with motors free) while keeping the
front of the robot clear during navigation.

An Orbbec DaBai DC1 camera is mounted in an eye-in-hand configuration on
`gripper_base` (25 mm along +X, 56 mm above the approach axis, 80 mm behind the
fingertips `tcp_link` at +0.1358 m from `link6`). Because the DaBai DC1 optical
depth minimum is ~0.30 m (manufacturer specification), close-range wrist
perception (< 0.30 m) relies on calibrated RGB monocular geometry and ray-plane
intersection (Section 4.2) rather than raw wrist depth.

2.3 Pan-Tilt Head, LiDAR, and Audio Hardware
At the top of the torso assembly (`head_base_z` = 0.12 m above the prism), a
2-DOF pan-tilt unit driven by two Robotis Dynamixel XL430-W250 servos over a
1 Mbaud U2D2 interface (Protocol 2.0, IDs 1 and 2, multi-turn extended position
mode with software turn-unwrapping) carries an Intel RealSense D435 RGB-D
camera operating at 640x480 @ 15 Hz (firmware 5.17.0.10) for person pose
estimation, biometric face recognition, seat occupancy analysis, and 3D floor
pointing-gesture localization. Planar obstacle detection and localization are
handled by a front-mounted Hokuyo UST-05LX 2D LiDAR (270 deg FOV, 0.06–5.0 m
range, 40 Hz) mounted at x = 0.22 m, with its base 0.32 m above the floor and
its scanning plane at 0.38 m height (measured on the physical robot). Voice
capture uses a directional supercardioid RØDE VideoMic GO II USB shotgun
microphone mounted on the head assembly.

2.4 Distributed Onboard Compute and Wired Networking

operation):
- Primary Host (CHUWI LarkBox S NUC): Intel Core i3-1220P (10 cores / 12

  the protected Nav2/AMCL navigation stack, Hokuyo LiDAR driver (via dedicated
  USB-Ethernet interface), hoverboard UART driver, ESP32-S3 elevator controller,
  RealSense D435 driver, HRI state machines, mission compiler/executor, MoveIt 2
  planning server, and facial display/TTS.
- Secondary Host (NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano 8 GB Super Dev Kit): 6-core Arm
  Cortex-A78AE CPU (25 W power mode, 1.344 GHz), Ampere GPU with CUDA 13.2 and
  TensorRT 10.16.2, 7.4 GiB shared RAM, Ubuntu 24.04.4 / L4T R39.2.1 (JetPack
  7.2.1, kernel 6.8.12-tegra). Managed by the `jetson-head.service` systemd
  unit, it runs the 50 Hz SocketCAN (`gs_usb` @ 1 Mbps) Piper arm controller,
  20 Hz Dynamixel pan-tilt node, Orbbec DaBai wrist stream (20–30 Hz), FP16
  TensorRT YOLOv8 inference node, and streaming Whisper ASR node.

The two computers communicate over a direct wired 1 Gbps Ethernet link (measured
RTT 0.2 ms, throughput >= 660 Mbps, NTP clock offset -11.9 ms; historical
measurement on hardware). CycloneDDS is configured in explicit unicast mode
with multicast disabled, completely avoiding WiFi hotspots in compliance with
RoboCup@Home RuleBook §3.2.8.

3. Distributed ROS 2 Software Architecture, Safety, and Simulation
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 Single-Owner Joint States and Hardware Safety Interlocks
In multi-process robotic systems, publishing synthetic zero joint positions when
an actuator is unpowered corrupts TF trees and motion planners. Base@Home
enforces a strict single-owner `/joint_states` architecture (`hoverboard_base`
URDF): `piper_arm_controller` publishes `/arm/joint_states` (50 Hz),
`pan_tilt_node` publishes `/pan_tilt/joint_states` (20 Hz), and `torso_state`
publishes `/torso/joint_states` (5 Hz), aggregated by `joint_state_publisher`,
while commanded arm targets are separated onto `/arm/joint_states_cmd` for
visualization only.

All actuator drivers implement hardware-level safety guards:
- `piper_arm_controller` (`jetson_head/scripts/piper_arm_controller.py`): Starts
  with `auto_enable=False`. It refuses to enable motors unless fresh CAN
  feedback frames have arrived within `feedback_timeout_s = 0.5` s, preventing
  the SDK from snapping to a zero-radian setpoint when unpowered. Any trajectory
  received while disabled is discarded, disabling the arm immediately flushes
  active trajectories, and commands outside the URDF joint limits (`j1: +-154`,
  `j2: 0..195`, `j3: -175..0`, `j4: +-110`, `j5: +-80`, `j6: +-180` deg) are
  rejected in full rather than clipped. Closed-loop execution is exposed via a
  `FollowJointTrajectory` action server (`/arm/follow_joint_trajectory`) that
  aborts if tracking error exceeds `fjt_path_tolerance_deg = 15.0` deg and
  confirms goal arrival against measured CAN feedback within
  `fjt_goal_tolerance_deg = 2.0` deg (`JETSON_VERIFIED` for disabled-state goal
  rejection; 32 unit tests).
- `pan_tilt_node` (`jetson_head/src/pan_tilt_controller`): Starts with
  `auto_torque=False`, applies multi-turn unwrapping on the tilt axis (`homing
  offset -2212`, `pan_center_tick 2059`), and limits PWM (`pwm_limit = 600`,
  `position_p_gain = 450`) to prevent mechanical overload against hard stops.
- `torso_state` (`src/base_athome_bringup/base_athome_bringup/torso_state_node.py`):
  Opens the ESP32-S3 serial port with `DTR=False` and `RTS=False` to prevent
  microcontroller resets, polls telemetry (`?`) at 5 Hz, and refuses to publish
  `/torso/joint_states` or execute `/torso/goal` motions unless the elevator is
  calibrated (`calibrated=True` or referenced against the top limit switch with
  persisted `max_steps` travel) and `allow_motion:=True` is explicitly set.

3.2 Multi-Fidelity Simulation (`base_athome_sim`)
To enable deterministic testing without risking hardware or interfering with
the real robot (`ROS_DOMAIN_ID=10`), `base_athome_sim` (`src/base_athome_sim`)
provides a complete interface-compatible simulation in `ROS_DOMAIN_ID=42` (or
`77` for isolated unit tests) activated by `ATHOME_SIM=1`. It emulates the
Piper arm (cosine-profile trajectory interpolation and `FollowJointTrajectory`
server), pan-tilt head (90 deg/s), request-indexed Whisper ASR protocol (with
mute-paused timers), RealSense RGB-D streams, synthetic wrist-camera projection
of target objects using live arm forward kinematics, Hokuyo raycasting over the
occupancy grid, and a mock `navigate_to_pose` action server (or optional Gazebo
Harmonic physics via `ros_gz_bridge`). Simulation configurations and face
databases are isolated under `~/.local/share/base_athome/sim/`.

4. Key Technical Contributions
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 Deterministic Voice-to-Mission Compilation, Supervised Execution, and
    Streaming VAD Speech Pipeline
A central design decision in Base@Home is that free-form outputs from
probabilistic language models must never directly command robot actuators.
Instead, spoken and textual user instructions are processed by a three-stage
deterministic pipeline (`src/base_athome_skills/base_athome_skills/mission/`):

1. Request-Indexed Streaming ASR (`jetson_head/src/audio_asr/` and
   `src/base_athome_perception/base_athome_perception/voice_client.py`):
   Earlier fixed-window (6 s) recording suffered from truncated sentences,
   dropped low-volume speech, and cross-talk between consecutive dialog turns.
   Our redesigned `whisper_node` continuously captures 48 kHz audio from the
   RØDE shotgun microphone, downsamples to 16 kHz in 32 ms blocks, and runs
   streaming Silero VAD (0.34 ms per block on the Orin Nano CPU) alongside a
   continuous background noise floor estimator. Each listen request carries a
   unique `id`, a context-specific `initial_prompt` (biasing Whisper toward
   either official guest names/drinks or valid map places/objects), and
   configurable endpointing parameters (`start_timeout = 8.0` s,
   `end_silence = 1.1` s, `max_speech = 15.0` s, plus pre-roll buffering and a
   weak-voice gate at Silero >= 0.12 with RMS >= 4x noise floor). While the
   robot speaks (`/voice/mute = True`), incoming audio blocks are zeroed and
   the `start_timeout` countdown is paused rather than resetting the request.
   Transcription runs in a serialized queue using `faster-whisper` (`base.en`
   int8 on CPU, beam size 3) with hallucination, prompt-echo, and repetition-loop
   filters, always emitting an explicit terminal event (`ok`, `no_speech`,
   `rejected`, `cancelled`, or `error`) on `/voice/result`.

2. Bilingual Deterministic Command Compiler (`command_parser.py`):
   The compiler (`compile_command`, 592 lines, pure Python) parses compound
   natural-language requests in English and Spanish (e.g., *"Go to the kitchen,
   find a bottle, and bring it to the table; if you cannot find it, go back
   home"* or *"Take out the trash"*) into a structured `Plan` DAG. Rather than
   guessing when faced with ambiguity, the compiler enforces strict semantic
   rules against the live semantic map (`places.py`):
   - It automatically injects required implicit preconditions: moving the arm
     to `pre_grasp` before `detect_object`, requiring `detect_object` before
     `pick_object`, and expanding `"take out the trash"` into the 10-step
     pointed-bag pickup, elevator coordination, navigation to `trash_drop`, and
     floor release sequence (`_fetch_pointed_bag` + `_drop_bag`).
   - It supports conditional branches (`if: {step: <id>, state: FAILED |
     SUCCEEDED}`) for `"if you cannot find it..."` clauses, setting the
     preceding detection step to `on_failure: continue` and guarding subsequent
     actions on `SUCCEEDED`.
   - It resolves numbered map objects (`#1`, `#2`) and furniture approach poses
     from `semantic_map.yaml`, explicitly rejecting unknown locations, multiple
     ambiguous objects of the same class (asking the user to specify the object
     number), objects whose approach point falls inside a furniture polygon, or
     skills marked `UNAVAILABLE` (`find_person`, `follow_person`,
     `guide_person`, `deliver_object`, `answer_question`).
   - Every compiled plan automatically appends a safety recovery block (`speak`
     failure notification, `set_torso` to 0.0 m if the elevator was used, and
     `return_home`) and sets `success_if` to the final utility-producing step.

3. Pre-Execution Validation, Spoken Confirmation, and Supervised Execution
   (`plan.py`, `executor.py`, `node.py`, `commander_node.py`):
   Before any motor moves, `validate_plan()` verifies step count (<= 50), unique
   IDs, skill availability in `SkillRegistry` (`skills.py`), required/optional
   arguments, retry bounds (0..5), timeout bounds, forward/backward jump targets,
   and semantic place existence. In voice mode (`commander_node.py`), the robot
   synthesizes a natural-language summary of the compiled plan (`plan_to_speech`
   in `voice_dialog.py`) and asks *"Should I do it?"*; execution only starts if
   `parse_yes_no()` confirms an affirmative response, while saying *"stop"* at
   any time triggers `/mission/cancel`. During execution, `MissionExecutor`
   runs each step in a monitored thread with a `SkillContext` cancellation
   event (`CANCEL_GRACE_S = 6.0` s, `MAX_JUMPS = 20`), ensuring that canceling a
   navigation step aborts the active Nav2 goal and waits up to 5.0 s for Nav2
   confirmation before returning control. Furthermore, `mission_executor`
   refuses to initialize on `ROS_DOMAIN_ID=10` in simulation mode (`preflight`
   check before `rclpy.init`) and requires `supervised:=True` (plus
   `allow_manipulation_real:=True` for arm/elevator actions) in real mode.

4.2 Pointed-Bag 3D Perception, Prismatic Torso Coordination, and Side-Mounted
    6-DOF Manipulation
Domestic tasks such as taking out the trash or picking up dropped items require
perceiving objects on the floor pointed at by a user and grasping them without
colliding the robot chassis or arm with the ground. Base@Home addresses this
through a tightly coupled head-torso-wrist pipeline (`bag_finder.py`,
`bag_task.py`, `arm_manipulation.py`, `wrist_detector.py`, `wrist_geometry.py`):

1. 3D Pointing-Gesture Raycasting and Open-Vocabulary Bag Detection
   (`src/base_athome_perception/scripts/bag_finder.py` and `bag_task.py`):
   When `mission_executor` invokes `approach_person`, `bag_finder` sweeps the
   pan-tilt head across `(0, +30, -30, +60, -60)` deg, detects the user with
   YOLOv8n-pose (`conf >= 0.45`), visually tracks the torso center using a
   proportional deadband controller (`gain = 0.6`, `deadband = 3.0` deg,
   `max_step = 8.0` deg), and computes the median 3D person position in the
   `map` frame using aligned RealSense D435 depth (median over a 9x9 pixel
   patch). If the user is farther than `PERSON_NEAR_M = 2.2` m, the robot
   navigates to stop at `PERSON_STOP_M = 1.5` m facing the person.
   Next, in `find_pointed_bag`, the robot asks *"Please point at the bag."* and
   deprojects the 3D coordinates of the shoulder, wrist, and hip keypoints
   (`MIN_KP_CONF = 0.4`) into `map`. A valid pointing gesture is recognized when
   the arm vector separates from the torso downward vector by at least
   `MIN_POINT_ANGLE_DEG = 35.0` deg (`is_pointing()`), and the 3D ray from
   shoulder through wrist is intersected with the floor plane `z = 0` up to
   `MAX_POINT_RANGE_M = 6.0` m (`pointed_floor_point()`). Once 3 coherent
   pointing rays are accumulated, their median `(x, y)` floor intersection is
   computed, the pan-tilt head turns to look directly at that floor spot, and
   `BagDetector` (`wrist_detector.py`) runs open-vocabulary `YOLOv8s-worldv2`
   detection. Crucially, configuring YOLO-World with explicit background
   distractor classes (`["black garbage bag", "trash bag", "plastic bag", "bag",
   "person", "chair", "table", "bottle", "box", "wall", "floor"]`) raised bag
   detection recall on a 24-image offline test set from 4/24 (standard COCO) and
   6/24 (bag-only classes) to 17/24 by preventing low-confidence suppression.
   The bag closest to the pointed ray within `MAX_BAG_FROM_RAY_M = 1.0` m is
   selected, recording both its `(x, y)` center and the 3D height `z` of its
   upper handle edge (`top[2]`).

2. Collision-Free Base Docking and Prismatic Elevator Lowering (`bag_task.py`,
   `node.py`):
   Because Nav2 enforces a `robot_radius` of 0.44 m (`xy_goal_tolerance = 0.18`
   m) and the Hokuyo LiDAR beam is at 0.38 m height (unable to see low bags),
   the robot cannot simply drive up to a bag with the arm hanging down. Instead,
   with the elevator at the top (`torso_lift_joint = 0.0` m), the arm first
   moves to a collision-checked floor pre-grasp configuration
   (`FLOOR_PREGRASP_DEG = [-15.4, 161.1, -112.4, 83.9, 78.0, -50.9]` deg, or a
   user-taught `pre_grasp_floor`), which places the grasp point at
   `(REACH_X = 0.58, REACH_Y = 0.12, REF_OBJECT_Z = 0.933)` m in
   `base_footprint`, leaving a verified clearance (`base_clearance()`) of at
   least `MIN_BASE_CLEARANCE_M = 0.05` m ahead of the 0.24 m front chassis edge.
   Nav2 then navigates to a stand-off pose delayed by `DOCK_DISTANCE_M = 0.40` m
   along the approach line (`approach_bag`), after which `dock_bag` advances
   straight at `DOCK_SPEED_MPS = 0.08` m/s using odometry while monitoring the
   front +-20 deg LiDAR sector (`dock_verdict()`) to stop immediately if any
   tall obstacle appears closer than `dock_stop_range()`. With the elevator
   still at the top, `remember_bag` uses the wrist camera to refine the bag's
   `(x, y, z)` coordinates in `base_link` by intersecting the optical ray
   passing through the upper 15 % of the 2D bounding box (`grasp_box_fraction =
   0.15`) with the horizontal plane `z = plane_z` measured by the head camera
   (`ray_plane_point()` in `wrist_geometry.py`), while rejecting self-detections
   of the robot's own gripper/chassis in the bottom 30 % of the image
   (`OWN_ROBOT_BAND_FRAC = 0.70`) or detections farther than
   `BAG_MEMORY_TOLERANCE_M = 0.25` m from the head estimate. Finally,
   `set_torso` lowers the NEMA 23 elevator by `torso_for_object(z_object) =
   max(-0.58, min(0.0, z_object - 0.933))` m so the open gripper descends
   directly to the handle height.

3. Side-Mounted Kinematics, Multi-Orientation MoveIt 2 Planning, and Grasp
   Confirmation (`arm_manipulation.py`, `piper_kinematics.py`,
   `base_athome_moveit_config`):
   Analytical forward kinematics using the AgileX SDK Modified Denavit-Hartenberg
   parameters (`dh_is_offset = 1`) was cross-validated against the expanded URDF
   across 2,000 random configurations, yielding a maximum discrepancy of
   0.15 mm in position and 0.004 deg in orientation. In MoveIt 2 (TRAC-IK with
   `Distance` solve type, 0.5 s timeout, OMPL planner, velocity/acceleration
   scaling 0.3, and a 2,000-sample Allowed Collision Matrix checking 117
   arm-body and 18 internal arm pairs), straight-line Cartesian advances from
   the taught `pre_grasp` pose with fixed orientation reach ~15–20 cm. To
   extend the effective workspace, `_grasp_core()` evaluates 17 candidate tool
   orientations (0 deg plus tilts of 8, 16, 24, and 30 deg around the lateral
   and vertical axes perpendicular to the approach vector) using a fast
   collision-free IK pre-filter at the grasp point before computing the 10 cm
   approach IK, Cartesian approach line, and joint-space trajectory. This
   reduces planning time on unreachable targets from 38 s to ~7 s and extends
   reachable advance to 22–25 cm (~10 s planning time). Temporal flicker in
   wrist detections is mitigated by `TargetTracker` (IoU continuity + 5-frame
   median width smoothing) and an `object_hold_s = 8.0` s target latch in
   `base_link` (validated in simulation under 80 % random frame dropout with
   4/4 grasp completions). After closing the gripper to `GRIPPER_CLOSE_MM =
   50.0` mm and lifting 3 cm, `pick_blocking()` verifies physical grasp capture
   by checking that the measured CAN gripper width exceeds `GRIPPER_CLOSE_MM +
   5.0` mm; if the fingers close completely (`<= 55 mm`), it reopens the
   gripper and reports an explicit `grasp_empty` failure.

4.3 Protected Differential Navigation, Semantic Map Layer, and WebXR
    Teleoperation
1. Differential-Drive Navigation (`hoverboard_base`,
   `base_athome_navigation`):
   The navigation stack uses `map_server`, AMCL (`DifferentialMotionModel`,
   `likelihood_field`, 500–2,000 particles), `SmacPlanner2D` (0.05 m resolution
   global/local costmaps, `robot_radius = 0.44` m, `inflation_radius = 0.59` m),
   and a `RotationShimController` (`angular_dist_threshold = 0.785` rad,
   `rotate_to_goal_heading = True`, `0.70` rad/s in-place rotation) wrapping
   `RegulatedPurePursuitController` (`desired_linear_vel = 0.38` m/s,
   `min_lookahead_dist = 0.58` m > `track_width = 0.5620` m to eliminate
   lateral oscillation). The `hoverboard_driver` transmits `0xABCD` XOR-checked
   serial frames at 50 Hz with a 0.5 s `cmd_vel` watchdog, anti-stiction floors
   (`min_speed_raw = 75`, `min_steer_raw = 165`), and 22-byte closed-loop Hall
   odometry integration.

2. Semantic Map Layer and Live Map Synchronization (`map_zone_editor.py`,
   `semantic_objects.py`, `nav_map_sync.sh`):
   Without modifying the protected Nav2 parameters, an application-layer
   semantic representation (`semantic_map.yaml` and `waypoints.yaml`) stores
   named waypoints, polygonal furniture zones, numbered objects with automatic
   stand-off approach poses outside furniture polygons, and virtual obstacles,
   synchronized to `map_server` via `/map_server/load_map`.

3. Zero-WiFi WebXR Teleoperation (`jetson_head/scripts/quest_teleop_node.py`,
   `quest_cartesian_core.py`):
   To collect manipulation demonstrations without violating the competition
   no-hotspot rule, a Meta Quest 2 headset connects via USB to the Jetson using
   an `adb reverse` tunnel (`localhost:8765`, IPv4/IPv6). A 60 Hz WebXR page
   streams 6-DOF controller poses with a dead-man clutch (`X` button) to a 25 Hz
   node implementing One Euro filtering and adaptive damped least-squares (DLS)
   differential IK (`ik_damping` adapting from 5 far from singularities to 80
   near the wrist singularity at HOME, reducing opposite `j4/j6` wrist flips
   from 40 deg to 7 deg) with uniform joint velocity scaling (20 deg/s arm,
   40 deg/s wrist, `max_lead_deg = 5.0` deg).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. Conclusions and Future Work
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
We have presented the hardware and software architecture of Base@Home, a
low-cost, open-source domestic service robot built on ROS 2 Jazzy for
RoboCup@Home OPL. By combining a deterministic bilingual mission compiler and
supervised executor with a streaming VAD speech interface, a 0.60 m prismatic
torso column, a side-mounted 6-DOF manipulator, and hardware-level actuator
safety guards, the platform provides a verifiable foundation for domestic
service tasks without relying on unconstrained LLM outputs in the control loop.

Immediate work prior to competition focuses on: (1) resolving the 12 V power
delivery stability on the pan-tilt U2D2 bus under torque load; (2) completing
supervised physical validation series (`HARDWARE_VERIFIED`) for the HRI
Receptionist challenge, pointed-bag trash disposal with the calibrated ESP32-S3
elevator, and tabletop object grasping following the 6-step OpenCV eye-in-hand
calibration protocol (`handeye.py`); (3) exporting `YOLOv8s-worldv2` to FP16
TensorRT on the Jetson Orin Nano; and (4) evaluating online SLAM for the
unmapped Restaurant environment. All source code, CAD/URDF models, firmware,
and test suites are publicly available at
`https://github.com/ShiiSuii/base_athome`.
